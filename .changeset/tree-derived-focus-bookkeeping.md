fix(tree): navigate the items that are in the DOM, and derive the tab stop instead of measuring it.

- `Tree` no longer reads its items into state from an effect after every `children` change. The navigation keys read them from the DOM when they are pressed, so items that were added, removed or moved since the last render are navigated in their current order, and the render that effect caused is gone.
- The tree is a single tab stop (roving tabindex). The item that was focused last, or mounted as `selected`, holds it for as long as it is registered and not inside a collapsed ancestor. Until then — and again while that item is unmounted or hidden by a collapsed ancestor — the tree element itself is the tab stop (`tabindex="0"`) and passes keyboard focus on to the first item that can take it. A `selected` item that cannot take focus where it is (hidden, `inert` or disabled, or rendered through a `linkAs` that does not pass the item's `tabIndex` on) does not take the tab stop away from the tree element. The first item used to receive `tabindex="0"` one commit after mount, and a removed or hidden focused item left the tree unreachable by keyboard.
- While the tree element is the tab stop, a pointer press between the items leaves focus on it (and calls `onFocus`), and the navigation keys enter the items from there: `ArrowDown` and `Home` at the first, `ArrowUp` and `End` at the last visible item. The tree element stays the tab stop for as long as it has focus, also when an item becomes eligible meanwhile. A `tabIndex` prop applies to the tree element in that state only. Once an item is the tab stop the tree element is not focusable, and such a press moves focus out of the tree as before. Give the tree an accessible name (`aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`), as it can now hold focus.
- `focusedElement` of `useTree()` is the item that holds the tab stop and `null` while the tree element does; it used to be the first item before any interaction.
- `Home` and `End` move focus to the first and last visible item. The navigation keys leave keys typed into editable content inside an item alone, and `Enter` activates an item only when pressed on the item itself, not inside such content.
- Items with an `href` work like the others: tabbing onto one no longer breaks the arrow keys for it (the focused element may be the item's link, and the item is resolved from it), its children are shown while it is expanded (the group was always hidden) and are owned by its `treeitem` through `aria-owns`, and `ArrowLeft` from one of its children focuses its link.
- A tree rendered into another document (through a portal into an iframe) is navigated like any other: the tree recognises elements and the active element by their shape instead of with `instanceof` against its own realm's constructors, which did not recognise that document's elements (the keys skipped its link items and a click on an item did nothing).
